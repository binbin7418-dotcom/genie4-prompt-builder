How to Write a Genie 4 Prompt in Five Steps

A Genie 4 prompt is two texts, not one: the world seed you paste at the start, and the steering message you send mid-run. The five steps below build both. The free builder assembles them from dropdowns; this page explains what each choice is doing. For finished examples by job, see use cases.

Step 1 — Choose a concrete setting

Name a place with a shape to it: not “a village” but “a cliffside fishing village above a harbor”. Concrete settings give the model landmarks to keep consistent as you move. Add one signature detail — a lighthouse, a sleeping cat, a broken pier — that the world can be recognized by in every frame.

Weak: “a forest”. Strong: “a cathedral grove of giant redwoods with a fallen trunk bridge over a creek”.

Step 2 — Set weather and light

Weather and light decide mood and visibility more than any other ingredient. Pick a specific time (“blue-hour dusk”, “foggy dawn”) and one weather behavior (“rolling fog banks”, “first snow flurries”). If the weather will change during the run, don't put the change here — that's the turning event's job, in step 4.

Weak: “nice day”. Strong: “golden hour, long shadows, light drizzle starting”.

Step 3 — Plan the camera path

Decide how the viewer moves: a slow forward walk, a gentle aerial rise, a side-tracking shot, an orbit around the central landmark. A planned path beats “look around” because the model composes frames along the route — doorways, waterlines and viewpoints land where the path can see them.

Weak: “explore the area”. Strong: “slow forward walk along the waterline, ending at the harbor steps”.

Step 4 — Schedule the turning event

This is the step most prompts skip, and the one that matters most. Choose one change the world must react to — rain beginning, a character entering, the tide turning, lights switching on one street at a time — and give it a time: “at ~20 seconds”. Write it as its own line; in Genie 4 / Project Genie you send it as a steering message when the moment arrives, and the world simulates the consequences.

Weak: “something happens”. Strong: “at ~20s: rain begins and people pull awnings down”.

Step 5 — Finish with the audio bed

Genie 4 generates native audio, so the seed should say what the world sounds like: ambience of the place, two or three signature sounds, and whether there is music. “Waves, gulls and a distant bell, no music” beats “background sounds” every time. Dialogue, if any, goes in as a short quoted line with a named speaker.

Weak: “ambient audio”. Strong: “rain on canvas and wooden surfaces, a keeper calls out once: ‘Mind the step!’”

The assembled result

Put together, the five steps produce a prompt like this one:

World: a cliffside fishing village above a harbor, blue-hour dusk. Weather: rolling fog banks. Camera: slow forward walk along the waterline. Audio: waves, gulls and a distant bell, no music. Steering message (send at ~20s): fog rolls in and the far background disappears.

Two to four sentences of seed, one line of steering — that is the whole format. Paste the first part into Genie 4 as the opening description, send the second part at the stated time.

FAQ

Why do my Genie 4 worlds feel static?

Almost always a missing or vague turning event. A world seed alone produces a scene; the scheduled change — rain arriving, a character entering, lights switching on — is what makes it feel alive. Give the turning event a specific time and a visible consequence.

How many turning events should a Genie 4 session have?

One planned event per 20-30 seconds of run is a good rhythm. More than that and the world never settles between changes; fewer and it drifts back to feeling like a video. You can always improvise extra steering live.
